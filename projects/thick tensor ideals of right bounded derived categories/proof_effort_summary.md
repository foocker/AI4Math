# Proof Effort Summary

## 1. Problem Overview

**Problem (Remark 7.22, Matsui–Takahashi 2017).** Let $R$ be a commutative noetherian ring and $x \in R$ a nonzerodivisor. For a positive integer $d$ and non-negative integers $a_0, \ldots, a_d$, define

$$X(a_0, a_1, \ldots, a_d) = \bigoplus_{i \geqslant 0} \left(R/x^{a_0 i^d + a_1 i^{d-1} + \cdots + a_d}\right)[i].$$

Does the equality

$$\mathrm{thick}^{\otimes}\{X(a_0, \ldots, a_d) \mid a_0, \ldots, a_d \geqslant 0\} = \mathrm{thick}^{\otimes}\{X(1, 0, \ldots, 0)\}$$

hold for all $d \geqslant 3$? The case $d = 2$ was proven in Example 7.21 of the same paper.

**Classification:**
- **Area:** Commutative algebra / Homological algebra — thick tensor ideals in $\mathrm{D}^{-}(R)$, tensor triangular geometry.
- **Type:** Equality of thick tensor ideals; the non-trivial direction is showing every generator on the left belongs to the single-generator ideal on the right.
- **Difficulty:** Hard — an explicitly stated open research problem, unresolved for ~9 years. The $d = 2$ proof exploits a specific algebraic coincidence (2-fold even/odd splitting via Corollary 7.3) that does not directly generalize because $d!$ has odd prime factors for $d \geqslant 3$.

## 2. Final Proof Status

**Result: PASS — A correct proof was found.**

The proof establishes the affirmative answer for **all** $d \geqslant 1$, fully resolving Remark 7.22. The key strategy is:

1. **Generalize 2-fold splitting to $p$-fold splitting** (Lemma B): for any $p \geqslant 2$, a complex $\mathbf{X}(a_i)$ belongs to the thick tensor ideal generated by its $p$-fold sub-sampled parts $\mathbf{X}(a_{pi+r})$, $r = 0, \ldots, p-1$. This overcomes the fundamental obstruction of dividing by odd primes.
2. **Bottom-up induction on monomial degree**: starting from $\mathbf{X}(i^d) \in \mathcal{T}$, iterate finite differences (Lemma D) to produce $\mathbf{X}(\Delta^m(i^d)) \in \mathcal{T}$, subtract lower-order terms (Lemma C) using the inductive hypothesis, and apply $c$-fold splitting with $c = d!/k!$ to recover $\mathbf{X}(i^k)$.

The proof was verified with **78 out of 79 miniclaims passing**, zero failures, and one UNCERTAIN (a minor expository gap regarding the trivial case $k = d$ in the $c$-fold splitting step, which is mathematically inconsequential since $\mathbf{X}(i^d)$ is the generator of $\mathcal{T}$).

## 5. Key Mathematical Insights

### Core Discovery: $p$-fold Splitting (Lemma B)

The central insight that unlocks the general case is that Corollary 7.3's even/odd decomposition generalizes to **arbitrary $p$-fold splitting**. Given a complex $X = \bigoplus_{n \geqslant 0} M_n[n]$ with zero differentials, define $X^{(r)} = \bigoplus_{i \geqslant 0} M_{pi+r}[i]$ for $r = 0, \ldots, p-1$. Then $X \in \mathrm{thick}^{\otimes}\{X^{(0)}, \ldots, X^{(p-1)}\}$.

The proof constructs an auxiliary complex $Y_r = \bigoplus_{j \geqslant 0} R[(p-1)j + r]$, tensors it with $X^{(r)}$, and extracts the "diagonal" direct summand $Z_r = \bigoplus_{i \geqslant 0} M_{pi+r}[pi+r]$. This is the tool that enables "division by any integer $p$," overcoming the odd-prime obstruction.

### Coefficient Formula via Stirling Numbers (Lemma A)

The $m$-th forward finite difference of $i^d$ has the explicit expansion $\Delta^m(i^d) = \sum_{j=0}^{d-m} \binom{d}{j}\, m!\, S(d-j, m)\, i^j$, where all coefficients are non-negative integers. This non-negativity is essential: it ensures that the "lower-order remainder" $q(i) = \Delta^m(i^d) - \frac{d!}{k!}\, i^k$ has non-negative coefficients, enabling subtraction via Lemma C.

### Induction Architecture

The proof proceeds by induction on $k$ from $0$ to $d$, showing $\mathbf{X}(i^k) \in \mathcal{T}$:
- **Base case ($k=0$):** Extract $R/x$ as a direct summand of $\mathbf{X}(i^d)$, then tensor with $\bigoplus_{i \geqslant 0} R[i]$.
- **Inductive step ($k \geqslant 1$):** Apply $\Delta^{d-k}$ to $i^d$ (Lemma D), subtract lower-order terms (Lemma C + inductive hypothesis), and use $c$-fold splitting with $c = d!/k!$ (Lemma B) to recover $\mathbf{X}(i^k)$.

### Structural Observation

The proof reveals that the $d = 2$ case is not special — the same mechanism works uniformly for all $d$. The apparent obstruction at odd primes was an artifact of the limited toolkit (only 2-fold splitting) rather than a genuine mathematical barrier.
